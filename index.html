SPICE solves circuits; LTspice is the drawing program around it. Step through the build below. The netlist on the right is the text file SPICE actually reads: one line per part, listing the two nodes it connects and its value. Node 0 is ground, the reference every voltage is measured from.


      
SPICE understands prefixes: k = 10³, m = 10⁻³ (milli!), u = 10⁻⁶, n = 10⁻⁹, and MEG = 10⁶. Typing 1M gives 1 milliohm, not 1 megohm.

2 · .OP gives one answer; .DC sweeps a whole range

  
.OP (operating point) solves the circuit once: with 3 V in, Vout = 3 × 2k/(1k + 2k) = 2 V. .DC V1 0 10 0.1 re-solves it for every input from 0 to 10 V in 0.1 V steps (101 solutions), so you see Vout as a line. Drag the cursor (like clicking the V(vout) label in LTspice) to find the input that gives Vout = π.

  

    

      
      
      
      

    

    

      

      

    

  

  

    
Why V(vout) is a straight line
A divider’s output is always the same fraction of its input: Vout = V1 × 2/3. Plotted against V1 that is a line through zero with slope 2/3, shallower than V(top), which has slope 1.

    
Why the source current is negative
SPICE uses the passive sign convention for every part, including sources: it reports current flowing into the + terminal. A source pushes current out of its +, so SPICE shows it as negative. 3 V / 3 kΩ = 1 mA appears as −1 mA.

4 · Soldering: what a good joint looks like

  
Solder is a metal alloy that melts around 180–220 °C. The iron heats the pad and the component lead together; you feed solder into that hot joint (not onto the iron) and it flows around both. Press play to watch the sequence, then compare good and bad joints.

  

    

      
      

      

    

    

      

      
      

    

  

  

    
The five resistors (5% parts: 3 colour bands + gold)

    

    
Read bands from the end away from the gold band: digit, digit, multiplier (number of zeros). Example: red-red-orange = 2, 2, ×1000 = 22 kΩ.

  

  

    
Which side is which
Parts go on the component side (the side with “ATmega4809”) and are soldered on the solder side (the side with the white name box). J2 is the exception: it goes on the solder side, using the fixture to hold it straight.

    
J2: solder the middle pin first
One joint holds the header in place but can still be re-melted to straighten it. Check it sits flat, then solder the two outer pins after taking the board off the fixture.

    
Ground wire loop
A ~30 mm bare wire bent into a U with ~2.5 mm diameter, through the two GND holes, standing ~5 mm proud. Every later lab clips the scope and supply ground onto it.

5 · Verifying the board three ways

  
Check with the power off first (DMM in Ω), then power up from the programming tray and measure voltages. The final check sweeps VDD from 4 V to 6 V in LTspice and reads TP1 at the adapter voltage you actually measured.

  

    

      
      
      
    

    

      

      
If an unpowered resistance check disagrees with your calculation, find the problem (wrong resistor, missing joint, bridge) before powering up.
